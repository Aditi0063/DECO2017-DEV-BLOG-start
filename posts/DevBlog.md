# My first blog -  Interpretation of the brief & the start!

The BlaBla Corp brief uses words like “effortless sharing” and “tailored experiences” and aims to retain its users to come back for shared experiences and a valuable community. The brief wants us to answer what makes a community worth returning to in the first place and something that sets it apart from other platforms that already exist. 

One constraint that will affect the design decision is that there is no login page to build. Authentication is handled by the BlaBla Corp platform, which means every user arriving at the hub is a part of the main corporation. This is an important consideration as there are no specific guests to design around. The ideas can be as creative and unique, and they can be the centre of the experience. 

My initial ideas either target all kinds of creatives at different stages or target users who are into playing and reviewing indie games. These user groups have loyal fan bases who would love a community aspect as I am personally a part of these communities. Each of these ideas have a genuine case for why they need to have something beyond what Reddit or Discord already offer. 

Idea 1: Indie Game Review Community: 
- Users start with 8 word descriptions to describe an indie game 
- Others rate that description from 1-5stars 
If your description gets 15+ ratings averaging above 4 stars, then you permanently unlock a badge next to your name on every review 
Badge holders can post 30-word fuller and more descriptive reviews of games now 

Target Audience: Indie game enthusiasts and gamers, a loyal and passionate community that seek niche platforms for discussions. 

Strength: The trust mechanic for longer reviews must be earned through community validation, which incentivises quality of reviews and honesty. 

Risks: Badge - If the goal is set too high, nobody will earn one and the system will feel broken. If it is too low, the badge's value becomes meaningless. User testing would be able to resolve this problem. The rating system requires careful handling, users shouldn’t be able to rate their own descriptions and individual ratings shouldn’t be publicly visible to prevent social pressure. 

Accessibility: Rating system will be only using mouse clicks rather than keyboards. Find a way to make it accessible. 

Idea 2: Creative Project Idea Community: 
Users get to share their unique project ideas and how it turned out to the community 
Creative share the how they did the project - ungatekeeping creative process 
Helps young creatives understand the process and journey rather than be worried about the process
Can ask questions on the platform and the creator can answer to help them out 

Target Audience: Early stage creative like students, recent graduates and hobbyists who want to learn through making. 

Strength: Unique idea as most platforms only showcase the end result but this urges users to depict their process and display efforts. 

Risks: The complexity of the platform is a huge issue. A platform built around questions, answers and process documentation is complex. It risks being too broad to build. To start the community might be difficult as it will require a lot of documentation as well as courage to upload the process rather than just the result. Moderation on the type of questions asked - harmful or misleading questions? 

Accessibility: Images and Videos having alternative tags 

Both ideas have different requirements and present different technical challenges. The next step is to discuss our ideas as a group and commit to one direction before future design decisions.
